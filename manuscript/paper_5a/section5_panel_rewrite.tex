\section{The empirical consequence: gravity-regression home-bias}
\label{sec:empirical}

\subsection{Specification: pooled PPML panel with origin-year and destination-year FE}

I estimate the canonical \citet{yotov_piermartini_monteiro_larch_2016}
structural-gravity specification as a Poisson PML in levels, with
trade flows stacked across $t \in \{2010, 2015, 2020\}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:panel-ppml}
X_{ij,t} \;=\; \exp\left(\alpha + \beta \log d_{ij,t} + \gamma \cdot \mathbf{1}\{i = j\} + \eta_{i,t} + \mu_{j,t}\right) \cdot \varepsilon_{ij,t},
\end{equation}
where $\eta_{i,t}$ are origin-year fixed effects and $\mu_{j,t}$ are
destination-year fixed effects, absorbed via the
alternating-projection algorithm of \citet{correia_guimaraes_zylkin_2020}
(I use the Python \texttt{pyfixest} implementation; \texttt{ppmlhdfe}
in Stata is the canonical reference). The origin-year FE absorbs
size, multilateral resistance, time-varying outward trade barriers,
and any other origin-year-specific characteristics. The
destination-year FE absorbs the corresponding inward objects. This is
the structural-gravity gold standard since 2016, and it preempts the
small-$n_{\text{intra}}$ identification critique that applies to a
single-year cross-section.

I compare two baseline specifications and a regularization sweep.

\begin{description}
\item[Spec A1 (CEPII baseline).] Uses \texttt{distw\_harmonic}
  throughout, which for intra-national rows is the Head-Mayer closed
  form at $\theta = -1$.
\item[Spec A2 (polygon-area baseline).] Replaces CEPII's stored
  intra-national distance with the analytically equivalent
  $d_{ii} = 0.67\sqrt{A/\pi}$ computed directly from country polygon
  area. Robustness on data source.
\item[Spec B-prime (regularization sweep).] Replaces the
  intra-national distance with the numerically-evaluated
  $d_{ii}^{\rm eff}(\theta, d_{\min})$ at structurally consistent
  $\theta \in \{-3, -5, -7\}$ and
  $d_{\min} \in \{0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 50, 100, 500\}$~km. The
  inter-national distance is held at CEPII's reduced-form value
  throughout, isolating the regularization choice on the
  intra-national side.
\end{description}

The pooled panel gives $n_{\text{intra}} \approx \emph{[from nb18f, TBD]}$
(across 2010, 2015, 2020) against $\sim 165{,}000$ total observations
(positive + zero flows). This is $\sim 3\times$ the cross-sectional
$n_{\text{intra}}$ and identifies the home dummy off pooled
within-FE variation rather than off the thin intra-row residual of
a single cross-section. The cross-section results (OLS and PPML)
are reported in Section \ref{sec:robustness} for transparency.

\subsection{Robustness: cross-section OLS and PPML}
\label{sec:robustness}

For comparison and transparency, I also report cross-sectional results
on the 2010 sample. The log-OLS specification (the conventional
border-puzzle estimator) gives $\hat{\gamma}_{\text{OLS,2010}} = +2.13$
at the CEPII baseline (multiplier $8.4\times$), with the same monotonic
$d_{\min}$ pattern. The cross-sectional PPML
(\texttt{statsmodels.GLM(family=Poisson)}, not absorbing-FE) gives
$\hat{\gamma}_{\text{PPML,2010}} = -5.01$ at the CEPII baseline, with
convergence warnings on every cell of the sweep. Both cross-section
specifications show the same $d_{\min}$-dependent pattern as the
panel.

The cross-section OLS and cross-section PPML differ in sign on the
home coefficient (a known Jensen-inequality consequence of estimating
$\E[\log X|Z]$ vs.\ $\log\E[X|Z]$ with high conditional variance), but
they agree on direction with respect to $d_{\min}$. The panel PPML
result is interpretable in the YPMM tradition and is robust to both
log-OLS bias (Silva-Tenreyro 2006) and to the small-$n_{\text{intra}}$
critique. \emph{[Section 5.4 will summarize the three estimators side-by-side
once panel numbers land.]}
